Solar for business: when the numbers work

Businesses use power while the sun is up, which makes commercial rooftops some of the strongest candidates for solar. Here is what makes a site a good fit and what to check first.

Updated 20 October 20263 min readBy Solward

Key takeaways

  • Commercial solar works best where you use power in daylight hours and have a large, simple, unshaded roof.
  • EECA’s 2021 analysis of commercial-scale solar found paybacks around seven years, though costs and power prices have changed since.
  • Check the roof’s condition and structure, your lease, and your load profile before anything else.
  • Banks offer green and sustainability loans for business, so talk to your bank early.

Why daytime load matters

A business that runs on weekdays has demand and generation lining up. Power you use as it is produced is worth the most, because it replaces electricity you would otherwise buy. Exported power is credited at a lower rate, so a system sized to your daytime load usually makes a stronger case than one sized to the roof.

What makes a good commercial site

  • Large, simple roof planes with few obstructions
  • A roof in good condition, with years of life left and a structure that can carry the load
  • Daytime operating hours, such as workshops, offices, cool stores, retail and food production
  • Ownership, or a lease long enough to recover the cost, with clear make-good terms
  • A switchboard and supply with the capacity to connect a larger system

Understand your load before you size

The best sizing starts with half-hourly interval data, which your retailer or lines company can supply. It shows when your site actually draws power, so the system is sized to your real profile instead of a monthly average. It is also how we test a case before you spend anything. Our commercial packages show three common starting sizes.

Payback and the commercial case

EECA’s 2021 analysis of commercial-scale solar found paybacks around seven years. EECA is careful to note that installation costs and electricity prices have both changed since, so treat it as a guide rather than a forecast. We set out every assumption behind a commercial case separately, so your accountant can test them. How a solar asset is treated for tax depends on your circumstances, so speak to your accountant.

Paying for it

EECA’s advice is to talk to your bank early. Several major banks offer discounted green or sustainability loans for projects like solar. Our finance page lists them. Asset finance and leasing are other routes worth comparing.

Questions to ask any installer

  • Who designs the system, and who installs it?
  • Which assumptions drive the savings figure, and can I see them?
  • What happens to my roof warranty?
  • What monitoring and maintenance are included?
  • What happens if my premises change or I move?
