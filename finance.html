How solar finance usually works.

Four steps from quote to switch-on. The lender sets the terms, and we make sure the quote is ready for them.

01

Get a free quote

Lenders ask for a written quote from a professional installer, so start there.

02

Check with your bank

Ask about a top-up on your home loan. Equity and eligibility vary by bank.

03

Apply with your quote

Your bank approves the loan. Some release funds against the quote, others against the invoice.

04

Install and invoice

We install the system. Funds are released following your lender’s process.

What the major banks offer.

Home loan top-ups for solar and other energy upgrades. Figures below are as each bank published them on 20 October 2026.

Westpac

Greater Choices Home Loan

$50,000at 0%

Interest free for five years

  • For new or current Westpac Choices home loan customers, with no establishment fee.
  • Needs at least 20% equity afterwards for an owner-occupier (30% for an investment property), a Choices loan balance of at least $150,000, and your main income paid into Westpac.
  • Solar power systems and batteries are eligible. You supply a quote first, and invoices before funds are released.
  • Must be repaid within five years. Previously marketed as the Warm Up loan.
View on Westpac’s website

ANZ

Good Energy Home Loan

$80,000top-up

Special fixed rate for 3 years

  • A top-up to an ANZ home loan for eligible Good Energy upgrades.
  • ANZ’s page did not show the current rate when we checked, so ask ANZ for today’s figure.
  • After the three years, ANZ’s floating home loan rate applies.
  • ANZ lending criteria, terms and fees apply.
View on ANZ’s website

ASB

Better Homes Top Up

$80,000top-up

Fixed for 3 years

  • For existing ASB home loan customers, with at least 20% equity (30% for an investment property) after the top-up.
  • Solar panels, batteries and inverters are eligible when professionally installed. You need a quote or invoice no more than 60 days old.
  • ASB’s housing variable rate applies after the fixed period unless you re-fix.
  • Not available on construction loans or business loans.
View on ASB’s website

BNZ

Better Future home loan top-up

1%p.a.

Fixed for 3 years, up to $80,000

  • Advertised at 1% p.a. fixed for three years, with at least 20% equity in the property.
  • Solar panels, batteries and inverters are eligible, including planning and installation costs. A quote is needed.
  • A top-up fee of up to $100 (existing customers) or an establishment fee of up to $150 (new customers) may apply.
  • Previously called the Green Home Loan top-up. After three years you can re-fix, or the floating rate applies.
View on BNZ’s website

Kiwibank

Sustainable Energy Loan

$2,000contribution

On a variable-rate top-up

  • If you borrow more than $5,000, Kiwibank contributes up to $2,000 over four years towards the cost of the system.
  • For owner-occupied homes only. The system needs at least a 10-year manufacturer’s warranty.
  • A variable-rate loan over 7 to 10 years. Not available with offset or revolving home loans.
  • Existing customers can top up. New customers can include it when switching their home loan.
View on Kiwibank’s website

Other ways to pay.

Home loan top-ups are usually the cheapest route, but not the only one.

A

Personal loan

Usually unsecured, so you do not need home equity. Rates are typically higher than a home loan top-up, so compare the total cost.

B

Credit cards and store finance

Some providers offer interest-free terms. Check the rate that applies once the term ends, and any fees, before you commit.

C

Paying from savings

Avoids interest altogether. The payback range is the same, but there is no loan to repay, and you can add a battery later.
