How a home solar system works

A home solar system has fewer moving parts than most people expect. Here is how sunlight becomes the power in your kettle, and where the surplus goes.

Key takeaways

  • Panels make direct current (DC). An inverter turns it into the alternating current (AC) your home uses.
  • Your home uses solar power first. Surplus flows to a battery if you have one, and otherwise to the grid.
  • At night, or when demand is higher than output, you draw from the grid as normal.
  • Grid-connected systems switch off in a power cut unless they are designed with backup.

Step 1: panels collect daylight

Panels convert light into electricity. They need daylight rather than strong sun, so they work year-round, although output is lower in winter and on overcast days. Orientation and shading matter. A north-facing roof collects the most over a New Zealand year, east and west roofs still do well, and shade from trees or chimneys can reduce output noticeably.

Step 2: the inverter converts it

Panels produce direct current. The inverter converts it to alternating current at the voltage and frequency of the grid. It also manages the connection, watching the grid and shutting the system down if something is wrong. Inverters usually carry shorter warranties than panels, so ask about both.

Step 3: the switchboard shares it out

The inverter connects to your switchboard. Whatever your home is using at that moment is supplied by solar first, and the rest comes from the grid. Sometimes a switchboard needs work to take the new circuit safely, which we check at the site visit.

Step 4: surplus is stored or exported

If panels produce more than the home uses, the surplus goes into a battery if there is one, and otherwise to the grid, where your retailer credits it at its buy-back rate. Your meter measures power in both directions.

At night, and in a power cut

At night the panels produce nothing and the home draws from the grid, or from a battery. If the grid goes down, a standard grid-connected system shuts itself off for safety, so line crews are not put at risk. A system with a battery and a backup circuit can keep selected loads running.

Monitoring: see what it is doing

Most systems come with an app showing generation, use and export. We set it up at handover and walk you through what your first months of data will show.

Sizing: the short version

Size comes from how much power you use, when you use it, how much roof you have and your budget. We start from your actual bills and a site visit rather than a generic package. The system shaper on our Residential page gives an indicative starting point, and our home packages show three common sizes.

Terms worth knowing

kW
Kilowatt. How much power a system can produce or a device can draw at one moment.
kWh
Kilowatt-hour. An amount of energy, the unit on your power bill and the size of a battery.
Inverter
Converts the DC from panels into the AC your home uses.
Switchboard
Where electricity is shared out to the circuits in your home.
Export
Surplus solar power sent to the grid.
Buy-back
What your retailer credits you for exported power.
