Commercial

Make your roof work for your business.

Commercial load sits in daylight hours. That is where solar makes its clearest case, and where the numbers are easiest to hold to account.

Where a commercial system earns its place.

A typical weekday: your building draws power through the working day, and generation lands over the same hours.

Building load Solar generation Covered on site

The shaded area is generation your building uses as it is produced. For most commercial sites this is the largest part of the case, before export is counted.

What we put in front of a decision maker.

Enough to take to a board, a landlord or an accountant without translation.

Load analysis

Half-hourly interval data where it is available, so the system is sized against how the site actually behaves rather than a monthly average.

Scope and price

Equipment, structural allowances, electrical work, connection and commissioning, itemised and priced, with exclusions named.

Commercial case

Indicative return modelled on your own rates, with every assumption listed separately so your team can test them.

Delivery plan

Staging, access, roof and safety requirements, and how we keep the site operating through the install.

Sites that tend to suit solar.

Large, simple roof planes over a business that runs in daylight hours.

Light industrial and warehousing, trade and manufacturing premises, cool stores and food production, offices with steady daytime load, retail and hospitality with long trading hours, and horticultural or rural sites with irrigation and processing load.

Leasehold sites are workable too, and the lease term and make-good position are things we raise at the first conversation rather than at contract.
