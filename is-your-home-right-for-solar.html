Is your home right for solar? A practical checklist

Solar suits many New Zealand homes, but not every home at every moment. Work through these seven questions before you ask for a quote.

Key takeaways

  • Solar works best in electrified homes that use power in the daytime.
  • A roof that needs work in the next ten years should be fixed first.
  • Orientation and shade change output, but most roofs have something that works.
  • Know your comfort zone for upfront cost and payback time.

1. Do you mostly run electric appliances?

The more electric your home is, the more a solar system saves. If you still rely on gas, EECA suggests switching to electric hot water and heating ahead of going solar, so there is more daytime load for your panels to supply.

2. Do you use power during the day?

Solar only works in daylight, so the more electricity you use then, the more you save. If most of your use is outside daylight hours, a battery can help, though it adds cost. Shifting the dishwasher, washing and hot water into the day is a free way to improve the numbers.

3. Does your roof have enough space?

The bigger the system, the more unshaded roof it needs. As a rough guide, a typical home system takes somewhere between 20 and 50 square metres. Our home packages show the space each size needs.

4. Which way does it face, and what shades it?

In New Zealand a north-facing roof collects the most over a year. East and west roofs still work well, and flat roofs can be fitted with tilted frames. South-facing planes collect noticeably less. Trees, chimneys and neighbouring buildings can all cast shade, and we map it at the site visit.

5. What condition is your roof in?

If it needs replacing or major maintenance in the next ten years, EECA advises doing that first, to avoid the cost of removing and reinstalling panels. We check the roof and the framing under it before we quote.

6. Are you planning renovations?

Work that could change your roof or wiring should happen before you go solar, and be done with solar in mind. If an extension, a new EV charger or a heat pump is on the horizon, tell us. We can size the inverter and cabling so the addition is straightforward.

7. What is your financial comfort zone?

Solar can save money over time, but the value depends on your situation. EECA says the shortest payback periods are generally around 7 to 8 years, and can be 10 to 12 years in other situations. You need to be comfortable with an upfront cost and a wait of a few years. Our finance page covers the low-interest top-ups banks offer.

What we check at the site visit

  • Roof pitch, material, condition and the structure beneath it
  • Shading across the day and through the seasons
  • Your switchboard, meter and supply
  • Where the inverter and any battery could go
  • Safe access for installation

Consents and approvals

In October 2025 the government announced an exemption from building consent for rooftop solar. Connection to the network still needs approval from your local lines company, and we prepare that application as part of the job.

Not a fit yet?

That is a useful answer too. If your roof needs work or your home is not yet electrified, we will say so, and tell you what to do first.
